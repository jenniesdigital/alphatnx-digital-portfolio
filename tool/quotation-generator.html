What is a Quotation?

A price quotation (also known as a quote or tender estimate) is a formal business document provided by a contractor, agency, or supplier to a prospective client outlining the estimated or fixed cost for delivering specific goods, services, or project deliverables.

When a customer accepts and signs a quotation, it establishes mutual commercial agreement on scope and pricing before work starts, protecting both parties against unexpected cost overruns or scope misunderstandings.

Quotation vs. Estimate vs. Proforma Invoice

Understanding where a quotation sits in the procurement cycle prevents cash-flow missteps:

Estimate: An educated approximation of what a project might cost when final specifications or scope variables remain uncertain.
Quotation: A firm, specific commitment to deliver defined deliverables at exact prices if accepted within a set validity timeframe.
Proforma Invoice: A preliminary invoice sent to request mobilization deposits or confirm custom order specifications before final commercial invoicing.

Why Validity Periods Are Crucial for Nigerian Businesses

In Nigeria's dynamic commercial environment, providing open-ended quotes without an explicit expiration date is one of the quickest ways to lose money:

  • Foreign Exchange Shifts: If equipment, software licenses, or materials are pegged to foreign currency, an unhedged quotation accepted three months later can wipe out your entire profit margin.
  • Supplier Price Adjustments: Raw materials, shipping freight, and wholesale supplies fluctuate frequently. Stating Valid for 14 Days protects your ability to renegotiate if supply prices surge.
  • Urgency and Faster Decision-Making: Firm validity periods create a natural incentive for corporate procurement committees and business owners to finalize budgets and approve work promptly.

Key Components of a Professional Quotation

Every client-ready quotation should include:

  • Detailed Scope Breakdown: Explicit line items describing deliverables, phases, or quantities rather than an ambiguous single lump-sum figure.
  • Explicit Delivery Timelines: Expected completion benchmarks (e.g. "Turnaround: 10–14 business days upon initial mobilization").
  • Payment Schedule: Milestone deposit ratios (e.g. 70% upfront commitment, 30% upon final delivery) to ensure project liquidity.
  • Expiration / Validity Date: Clear statement of the period during which quoted prices remain locked.
  • Client Authorization Block: Signature and date fields for formal stakeholder approval.
