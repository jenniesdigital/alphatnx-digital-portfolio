The Difference Between Gross Margin and Markup

One of the most frequent pricing mistakes made by growing retail and e-commerce businesses is confusing Markup with Gross Margin. While both express profitability, they use completely different denominators:

Gross Margin: The percentage of selling price that remains as profit after subtracting the cost to deliver the product.
Gross Margin = (Selling Price - Cost) ÷ Selling Price × 100

Markup: The percentage added onto the cost price to arrive at the selling price.
Markup = (Selling Price - Cost) ÷ Cost × 100

If you buy an item for ₦10,000 and sell it for ₦15,000, your markup is 50% (₦5,000 ÷ ₦10,000). But your gross margin is only 33.3% (₦5,000 ÷ ₦15,000). If you offer a 40% discount during a sale because you thought your markup was 50%, you will actually sell at a net cash loss.

Where Nigerian Businesses Secretly Bleed Margins

In a high-friction retail environment, direct cost of goods is rarely where businesses fail. Profit leaks happen in the unmeasured unit costs:

  • Interstate Logistics & Rider Costs: When a customer in Abuja or Port Harcourt cancels a delivery upon arrival, the vendor absorbs two-way transit fees.
  • Foreign Exchange Replacement Gaps: Selling inventory at ₦20,000 when restocking will cost ₦22,000 next week due to currency shifts creates a phantom profit trap.
  • Manual Order Handling Overhead: Spending 15 minutes of staff time verifying a single bank transfer receipt eats into unit margin just as much as an ad click.
