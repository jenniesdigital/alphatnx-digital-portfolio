What is an Invoice?

An invoice is an official commercial document issued by a seller to a buyer that outlines products delivered or services performed, itemizes agreed prices, and formally requests payment under agreed terms.

Unlike a simple bank transfer screenshot or an informal WhatsApp chat message, a well-formatted invoice serves as a binding legal record of sale, establishes clear credit and delivery terms, and provides documentation for corporate tax filings, accounting audits, and dispute resolution.

Invoice vs. Receipt vs. Purchase Order: What's the Difference?

Many business owners in Nigeria use the terms "invoice" and "receipt" interchangeably, leading to costly record-keeping confusion:

Purchase Order (PO): Sent by the buyer to the seller requesting goods or services at specific prices before fulfillment begins.
Invoice: Sent by the seller to the buyer requesting payment once work is completed or products are ready for dispatch.
Payment Receipt: Sent by the seller to the buyer after cash or bank transfer has been received, confirming full settlement.

Key Elements of a Valid Business Invoice

To ensure your invoice looks professional and gets approved quickly by corporate finance teams, always include:

  • Unique Invoice Number: A chronological identifier (e.g., INV-2026-001) that allows both parties to reference transactions in bank statements.
  • Complete Business Identity: Your registered business name, official email, phone number, and physical office or dispatch address.
  • Client Details: The recipient's legal company name, contact person, and billing location.
  • Itemized Line Breakdown: Clear descriptions of items sold, quantities, and agreed unit rates instead of vague lump-sum charges.
  • Discounts & VAT: Clearly separated subtotal, applied promotional discounts, and statutory taxes (such as 7.5% Nigerian Value Added Tax where applicable).
  • Bank Account Instructions: Correct Nigerian bank name, 10-digit NUBAN account number, and exact account name to prevent transfer errors.
  • Payment Due Date: An explicit deadline (e.g. "Due on Receipt" or "Net 14 Days") rather than leaving payment timing open-ended.

How to Get Invoices Paid Faster in Nigeria

Late payments remain one of the biggest cash-flow killers for independent contractors and vendors. Follow these proven principles:

  • Send Invoices as Clean PDFs via WhatsApp & Email: Corporate clients need a PDF for accounting files, but sending a PDF copy directly to the decision-maker's WhatsApp ensures immediate visibility.
  • Require Upfront Deposits on Custom Work: Never deliver 100% of custom projects, manufacturing, or software development without securing an initial 50%–70% deposit invoice upfront.
  • Clearly State Payment Deadlines: Replace "Pay at your earliest convenience" with an exact due date. Specific deadlines establish professional accountability.
